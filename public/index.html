How earnings calls are processed
  1. Get the inputs. Earnings date and timing (before open or after close), reported vs estimated EPS, and the call transcript.
  2. Extract from the transcript. Chunk and retrieve the guidance, outlook, margin, and demand sections (Lab 3 approach), then return guidance, key metrics, tone, top Q&A theme, and what is new vs the last call as JSON.
  3. Score it. Guidance raised or cut, or an EPS surprise of 5% or more = impact 3. Guidance maintained with a tone shift = 2. In line with no change = 1. New information comes from what is new vs the last call.
  4. Assign it to the right trading day. After-close calls are matched to the next trading day's return; pre-market calls to the same day.
  5. Evaluate it. A call with a large report-day company-specific move should come out High.

Where the data comes from
  • Company, sector, and macro news: Alpha Vantage News & Sentiment API, filtered by ticker and date (Sep 11–17), plus economy topics for macro. Each article keeps its publisher, timestamp, and URL, which become the source links.
  • SEC filings: 8-Ks for NVDA, GOOG, JPM, XOM and 6-Ks for TSM from SEC EDGAR (data.sec.gov), filed in the window.
  • Earnings calls: transcripts from Alpha Vantage's earnings-call transcript endpoint; reported vs estimated EPS from its earnings endpoint.
  • Prices: daily prices for the five stocks, SPY, SMH, XLC, XLF, XLE from Alpha Vantage (~100 trading days for the regression).
  • Economic calendar: release dates and results from federalreserve.gov, bls.gov, census.gov, dol.gov, and eia.gov.
  • In this prototype: all text, numbers, and links are illustrative placeholders until the pipeline output is loaded.

Research priority, not a buy or sell recommendation. Returns and the market / sector / company split are computed in Python from price data (two-factor regression on ~100 prior trading days). Attention rule: company-specific move ≥ 2σ with new, high-impact company news = High; large move without news = Medium (unexplained); high-impact news without a large move = Medium; otherwise Low. Holdings with the same level are ordered by thesis relevance. The 2σ threshold means about 1 in 20 ordinary days crosses it by chance, so some flags may be noise. NVDA and TSM are large holdings in SMH, which understates their company-specific moves. Sector items tagged "Value chain" are news about a customer, supplier, or input (e.g., a cloud provider's capex for NVDA and TSM, or oil supply for XOM). Company items come from news, SEC 8-K filings (6-K for TSM, a foreign filer), and earnings calls; 8-K item codes inform the impact score. The broad-market part reflects overall market mood as well as economic releases.
